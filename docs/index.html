Performance vs. baselines

For: me, deciding whether to keep this strategy or change it

One line per trader over real time, not a single end-of-game number — the decision this supports is "am I actually beating a dumb strategy, and since when," which only a trajectory answers. The three organizer-flagged baseline traders (random buyer, random seller, random trader) are logged alongside me every tick, since the organizer only ever publishes a current snapshot, never a history, for any trader.

Risk (volatility)

For: me, judging whether a return is worth its swings

A rolling standard deviation of my own tick-to-tick returns, over time — not a single number, because risk itself can rise or fall as the strategy's exposure changes. A high-return, high-swing run should not look identical to a steady one on the performance chart alone.

Product-level signal

For: me, understanding what my own algorithm is actually doing

Three small multiples, not one shared chart — A, B and C trade on genuinely different price scales and (per the report) genuinely different dynamics, so forcing them onto one axis would hide more than it shows. Each panel shows the real price and the resulting signal over time, so a swing in the signal can be checked directly against the price move that caused it.

Decision log

For: me (and my report), explaining the trajectory, not just showing it

A table, not a chart — a manager (or an examiner) checking a specific past decision needs the exact reason, not a visual estimate, same reasoning this course used for every other "exact figures someone will act on" table.
